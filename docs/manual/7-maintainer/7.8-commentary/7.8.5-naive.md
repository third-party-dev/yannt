### Naive Wrapper

Pparse has a design philosophy of parsing everything as a state machine. It wants to parse everything itself as much as reasonable. Pparse should have no heavy or esoteric dependency trees. Everything in pparse should have positive control over the parsing and enable the principles of pparse.

When a technique or process does not meet these constraints, its automatically determined a "naive" technique (in the context of yannt). Its not to say that the technique is dump or inferior. Its to say that we remain ignorant of the behavior, constraints, and side effects of a specific upstream parser.

Even though a parser is collected as "naive", it remains incredibly valuable in many cases because its often the upstream maintained "normal" way to parse a piece of data. The naive plugins enable quick verification of pparse results and quick deployments of file parsing without having to generate the boilerplate code.

<div class="h3-style">Separate Plugins</div>

Each of the naive plugins are their own separate python packages. Unlike pparse plugins that minimize dependencies, naive plugins take on the big packages (e.g. pytorch). Since I do not want users to have to burden themselves with those heavy dependency trees to simply install and use yannt or some subset of `yannt naive`, each sub command is its own package and manages its own dependencies.

All of the (upstream) naive packages are kept in the `yannt/packages` folder.
